fix(jawp): normalise predictive_relevance onto a single axis

The diagnostic read max(0, 1 - (D/k)*w), not 1 - w. `ws_pred_error` was a
mean over the (N, k) workspace residual and `bg_pred_error` a mean over the
(N, D) full residual, so their ratio carried a spurious D/k factor. The zero
threshold therefore sat at w = k/D (chance level, 0.094 at D=64, k=6) instead
of at w = 1, and any workspace holding even a chance-level share of the
residual read exactly 0.0.

That is what made the "predictive_relevance collapses to 0" reading cited at
proofs/wsd.md:30, proofs/wsd.md:222, proofs/jawp.md:264, jawp.py and wsd.py
uninformative: it fired long before the workspace could have collapsed.

Both terms are now normalised over the same N*D axis, so the score is the
workspace's share of the residual energy and spans the full [0, 1].

The DIRECTION is unchanged, and was verified to be correct before changing
it: all five prose consumers and tests/test_host_reads.py:524 (zero residual
-> 1.0) already read low = dead, which is exactly what 1 - w produces.
Flipping the sign would have contradicted all six. See
.agent-notes/relevance.md for the decision and the evidence.

Diagnostic only: total_loss, dL/dz_pred and dL/dQ are unchanged, and the
metric is computed inside the existing torch.no_grad() block.

Mutation-verified: reintroducing the N*k mean turns 2 direction tests red;
flipping the sign to max(0, w) turns 5 red, including both endpoints.
